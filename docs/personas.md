## Persona 1: The Anxious First-Year

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Faith Wanjiru |
| **Age / Year of study** | 18, First year |
| **Service points used** | Registry (admission/clearance documents), Accommodation |
| **Tech comfort** | Comfortable with everyday apps (WhatsApp, social media), not a power user |

**Background**
New to campus and still learning how administrative processes work. Doesn't yet know which office handles what, or the informal norms other students have picked up (e.g. best time of day to go, what documents to bring).

**Goals**
- Understand what's actually happening in the queue without having to ask around or feel embarrassed for not knowing
- Avoid wasting a trip by finding out a service point is closed or the wrong one before arriving
- Feel confident she won't accidentally miss her turn

**Frustrations** *(validated — research/03-findings.md, first-/second-year respondent patterns)*
- Doesn't know how long a queue really is, and has to "just stay there and wait" with no progress information
- Wants to know whether a service point is busy before travelling there
- Uncertain whether a queue is actually moving when there's no visible progress indicator

**Quote** *(from usability testing, P01 — first-year, moderate tech comfort, limited service-point experience)*
> "I can see my position, but I'm not immediately sure what the QR code is for."

**How this app helps her**
- Onboarding Tutorial explains the process before first use
- Service Point Details screen shows requirements/hours before she commits to going
- Live Queue Status removes uncertainty about position
- Turn Notification reduces fear of missing her turn

## Persona 2: The Efficient Final-Year

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Brian Otieno |
| **Age / Year of study** | 22, Final year |
| **Service points used** | Finance (fee clearance), Registry (transcripts), occasional Clinic visits |
| **Tech comfort** | High — uses multiple apps daily, low tolerance for friction or repeated steps |

**Background**
Has used these service points many times before and knows the system well. Often juggling classes, part-time work, and final-year project deadlines, so time spent standing in line is time lost elsewhere.

**Goals**
- Join a queue remotely so time isn't wasted standing around
- Arrive exactly when needed, not earlier "just in case"
- Track history across repeated visits (e.g. multiple finance clearance trips)

**Frustrations** *(validated — research/03-findings.md, final-year respondent patterns)*
- Has to remain physically in the queue, unable to use that time productively for classes/work
- Doesn't trust a single notification channel — prefers SMS as a fallback when the app isn't open, or push+SMS together
- Wants confidence that a busy office is worth visiting before travelling there

**Quote** *(from usability testing, P04 — final-year, very high tech comfort, extensive service-point experience)*
> "I like being able to check the queue before travelling to the office."

**How this app helps him**
- Join Queue remotely frees up his time for other tasks
- Multi-channel Notification Preferences match his need for redundancy
- Ticket History/Queue History supports his repeated use across visits
- Digital Ticket with QR code speeds up verification at the counter
